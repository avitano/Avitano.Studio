Credit Cards.

Redesigned Bank Hapoalim's credit card experience: three functionally identical card products, sold on raw numbers and dense compliance text, became a visual-first, lifestyle-led selection flow.

Product design Banking

the challenge.

Leadership asked for higher conversion in the credit card application flow. The honest diagnosis came first: the bank's three card products, Visa Gold and Mastercard Gold among them, were functionally identical, just different brand skins on the same underlying product, while competitors offered real differentiation through loyalty perks and cashback. No amount of interface polish fixes a non-differentiated product, and that reframe shaped everything that followed.

01

The Commodity Trap

Cards were treated as generic financial utilities, forcing users to compare raw numbers and dry terms instead of emotional value.

02

Compliance vs. Experience

Heavy regulatory disclosures overloaded the interface with dense, intimidating text blocks, creating heavy cognitive friction.

03

The Aesthetic Deficit

The legacy interface lacked visual storytelling, failing to leverage the premium lifestyle appeal of high-tier cards, directly hurting conversion.

Coming soon

the strategy.

Design can't fix a business-strategy gap by itself, but it can raise the ceiling within one. He wouldn't commit to a conversion target unless the business side changed the product too, so the strategy focused on what was actually in reach: making the experience feel different even where the products weren't, and getting compliance out of the user's way.

01

Visual-First Framework

When core banking terms are identical, immersive visual identity becomes the primary decision-driver.

02

Lifestyle Immersion

Wrapping standard cards in rich contextual worlds, aviation, luxury travel, to evoke immediate emotional alignment.

03

Friction-Free Compliance

De-risked the regulatory overhead by moving dense legal disclaimers into progressive disclosure, keeping focus on the experience.
